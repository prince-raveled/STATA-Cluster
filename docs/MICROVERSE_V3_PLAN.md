## §2 Positioning (use in README, abstract, talks)

> MicroVerse turns multiverse analysis of microbiome differential abundance from a descriptive plot into calibrated inference. It enumerates defensible pipelines, weights them neutrally or by what the field actually does, controls error across both taxa and pipelines, tests whether findings transport across cohorts, and supports researchers before, during and after analysis: planning sample size, pre-registering the multiverse, reporting, reviewing claims and placing results against a public robustness atlas.

## §26 Phase 0: Neutral weighting and correctness

### 26.1 Why

In the default demo grid (IBD genus, Quick mode), 480 of the 532 specifications per FDR setting are rarefied (about 90%). The arithmetic reproduces the demo's published count exactly:

- 13 rarefaction variants (none, plus 4 depths × 3 seeds) × 4 prevalence levels × 4 transformations × 4 tests = 832;
- minus 156 logistic-on-non-raw (13 × 4 × 3);
- minus 144 further TMM-on-rarefied (12 × 4 × 4 = 192, of which 48 were already removed by the logistic rule);
- = 532 per FDR setting, × 3 FDR settings = 1,596.

Under one vote per specification, a taxon significant only after rarefying can reach ROBUST (about 90% of the weight), while one significant only without rarefying cannot pass FRAGILE (about 10%). The tiers therefore take a side in the rarefaction debate (McMurdie & Holmes 2014; Cameron et al. 2022; Schloss 2024) that MicroVerse says it does not take. Del Giudice & Gangestad (2021) warn that when alternatives are not truly arbitrary, multiverse-style analyses can mislead by burying meaningful effects among poorly justified alternatives. They propose classifying each decision as principled equivalence (Type E), principled non-equivalence (Type N) or uncertainty (Type U).

### 26.2 Weighting schemes (`app/core/weights.py`)

**Definition.** A specification's weight is the probability that an analyst who walks the pipeline in the v2 §9 order ends at that specification, choosing among the options that are still valid given earlier choices. Weights are products of conditional probabilities along a decision tree, so they are:
- always feasible, whatever the pruning;
- easy to explain;
- easy to audit.

**Decision nodes, in pipeline order:**
1. rarefy (yes/no);
2. depth, given rarefy;
3. seed, given depth;
4. taxonomic rank;
5. prevalence filter;
6. **analysis method**: the (transformation, test) pair, given the upstream choices;
7. FDR setting;
8. covariate subset (Covariate mode only).

Transformation and test are one node because pruning acts on their combination. Treating them separately creates structural zeros: TMM exists only without rarefaction, and raw counts with rank or t tests exist only with rarefaction after R8. Those zeros make per-fork balancing infeasible. In the IBD demo after R8, each rarefaction branch has exactly 10 valid method pairs.

| Scheme | Conditional probabilities | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `uniform` | w_s = 1 for every valid specification | v2 legacy; kept for comparison |
| `decision_tree` | Rarefy yes/no 1/2 each; uniform over the valid children at every other node | **v3 default.** "Rarefy or not" is the contested Type U decision; depth, seed, rank, filter, method pair and FDR are within-branch choices |
| `flat_tree` | As `decision_tree`, but the first node is the 5 rarefaction levels (none, min, 1000, 5000, 10000) with 1/5 each | Sensitivity |
| `practice` | Conditional frequencies estimated from the practice review (§28), Dirichlet-smoothed (+1 per valid child), restricted to valid children | Phase 2 |
| `custom` | User-declared conditional probabilities, stored in the sealed plan (§31.2) | Advanced |

**How weights are computed and used.**
- Weights are exact products; no iterative fitting is needed. Store them per specification in the run's specification table.
- **Per-taxon conditioning.** For each taxon, renormalise the weights over the specifications that actually tested it. This is the conditional probability given that the taxon was tested, and it is the weighted form of the v2 §15 denominator rule.
- **Seeds** split their depth's weight equally but remain separate specifications, so the v2 finding that 30–40% of rarefaction variance is the random draw stays measurable.

**Weighted tier inputs.**
- `frac_significant_w` = Σ w_s·1[significant] / Σ w_s, over tested specifications.
- `sign_consistency_w` is the weighted share agreeing with the weighted-majority sign.
- Tier rules and thresholds are unchanged until V8 (§33).

**Grid-composition panel on every run.** Shows:
- weight share of rarefied vs unrarefied specifications;
- weight share of each method pair family;
- the Kish effective number of specifications, n_eff = (Σw)² / Σw².

**Weight-stability flag.** A taxon is `weight-stable` if its tier is identical under `uniform`, `flat_tree` and `decision_tree` (and `practice` once available). Otherwise the UI shows which scheme changes it and how.

### 26.3 Pruning completion (`app/core/validity.py`)

| Rule | Removes or changes | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| R8 | Removes unrarefied + raw counts + {Wilcoxon, Welch, linear} | No library-size normalisation at all, so depth differences alone can create "differential" taxa (McMurdie & Holmes 2014) |
| R9 | Unrarefied + logistic (presence/absence) is kept only with log(total reads) as a covariate | Whether a taxon is detected depends on sequencing depth; the OMA MaAsLin 3 workflow adjusts for total reads for this reason |

**Defensibility register.** Every rule (v2 R1–R7 and R8–R9) and every fork level gets a `reason`, a `citation` and a Type E/N/U tag (Del Giudice & Gangestad 2021). These live in `docs/defensibility_register.json`, rendered as a table on `/about`.

Expected effect on the IBD demo: 520 specifications per FDR setting, 1,560 in total (from 1,596). The page shows both counts and the reason for every removal, as in v2.

### 26.4 Effect axes (`app/core/effects.py`)

- **Signed-z axis.** z_s = sign(native effect_s) · Φ⁻¹(1 − p_s/2) for every specification. v2 §14's harmonised log2FC cannot move with covariates by construction (its SD over 32 adjustment sets is exactly 0), so covariate-driven sign instability was invisible on the curve. The curve gets an axis toggle: harmonised log2FC (default) or signed z.
- **Pseudocount sensitivity.** Recompute the harmonised log2FC at the run pseudocount ×0.1 and ×10. Report the range and flag taxa whose sign changes.

### 26.5 Acceptance criteria

- On a hand-computed toy tree (with pruning), weights match exactly. Conditional probabilities at every node sum to 1 over valid children, and total weight sums to 1.
- Seeds receive equal weight within a depth.
- Per-taxon conditioning equals renormalisation over tested specifications, including taxa removed at some prevalence levels.
- Under `uniform`, every v2 output is byte-identical to v2 golden files (regression test).
- The IBD demo reports rarefied weight share of about 0.92 (`uniform`, after R8), 0.80 (`flat_tree`) and 0.50 (`decision_tree`).
- In the covariate-mode demo, signed z shows non-zero spread where the harmonised effect SD is 0.
- `docs/v3_weighting_note.md`: a one-page rationale with the maths, linked from `/about`.

## §27 Phase 1: Calibrated multiverse inference (N1)

### 27.1 Why

v2 tiers are descriptive thresholds (80%, 95%, 30%). The general multiverse literature has moved to inference:
- specification-curve inference (Simonsohn et al. 2020);
- Post-selection Inference in Multiverse Analysis (PIMA; Girardi et al. 2024), built on sign-flipping score tests (Hemerik, Goeman & Finos 2020), which combines all reasonable models and gives strong family-wise error control over specifications;
- its mixed-model extension PIMAX (Andreella & Vesely 2026);
- e-values for FDR control under arbitrary dependence between analyses (Rognon-Vael & Rossell 2026);
- minP adjustment for researcher degrees of freedom (BMC Med Res Methodol 2024).

None of these handles the structure of microbiome DA: hundreds of taxa × thousands of pipelines, heterogeneous tests (rank, linear, logistic, compositional), and compositional preprocessing. Solving that two-level multiplicity problem is MicroVerse's methodological contribution.

### 27.2 Hypotheses and claims

- For taxon j and specification s: H_js means no group difference under specification s's estimand. Different transformations target different estimands; that is expected and stated.
- Family F_j = {H_js : s tested j}. Global null H_j = ∩_s H_js.

Supported claims:
- **(a) Discovery.** Reject H_j (the taxon differs under at least one defensible pipeline), with FDR ≤ q across taxa.
- **(b) Certified robustness.** Within a discovered taxon, the set of specifications where H_js is rejected with error control. Its weighted share is the certified robustness CR_j.

### 27.3 Procedure (`app/core/inference.py`)

1. **Precompute** every preprocessed matrix (rarefy × collapse × filter × transform). All v2 preprocessing is label-independent: TMM factors, prevalence filters, rarefaction and CLR use all samples. Labels therefore enter only at the test step. This is what makes calibration affordable.
2. **Draw permutations.** Draw B group-label permutations, stratified by a batch column when supplied. Default B = 2,000 on the web; 20,000 offline. Store the permutation matrix G (B × n, 0/1) once per run with its seed.
3. **Vectorised statistics** for observed and permuted labels, per preprocessed matrix X (n × m):
   - Wilcoxon: ranks R computed once per taxon (tie-corrected variance is constant across permutations); rank sums = G·R.
   - Welch and linear: group sums G·X and sums of squares G·X².
   - Logistic (presence/absence): score test via G·P, with the R9 depth covariate handled by a residualised score.
   - Convert each statistic to signed z through its own parametric p-value. Validity comes from permuting the maximum; the scaling affects only power.
4. **Stream.** Keep, per permutation and taxon, the running max|z| over specifications (a B × m array) and the running weighted significance share. Memory is O(B·m), not O(B·S·m).
5. **Family p-value** (Westfall & Young single-step maxT): p_j = (1 + #{b : M_j^b ≥ M_j}) / (B + 1). When fewer than 10 permutation maxima exceed M_j, fit a generalised Pareto tail to the maxima (Knijnenburg et al. 2009). Report the raw and tail-fitted values.
6. **Discovery across taxa.** Apply BH at q to {p_j} (default). BY and e-BH (p-to-e calibration; Wang & Ramdas 2022) are dependence-robust options, following Rognon-Vael & Rossell (2026), who found p-to-e calibration the most powerful e-value route in multiverse settings. This gives the selected set S, with R = |S| and m taxa tested.
7. **Within selected taxa.** Compute specification-level adjusted p̃_js from the same maxT distribution. Reject where p̃_js ≤ q·R/m (Benjamini & Bogomolov 2014, selective inference on families).
8. **CR_j** = Σ_{s rejected} w_s / Σ_{s tested} w_s, using the §26 weights. Also record sign agreement among the rejected specifications.
9. **CERTIFIED ROBUST** requires all three: j ∈ S, CR_j ≥ 0.8 and sign agreement ≥ 0.95. These thresholds mirror v2 ROBUST so the two labels are comparable, and are pre-registered in §33.
10. **Report** p_j, q_j, CR_j and the v2 descriptive tier side by side. Never collapse them into one number.

**Assumptions, stated on the page in plain language.**
- Permutation validity needs exchangeability of samples under the null.
- Per-taxon validity of maxT relies on subset pivotality (Westfall & Young 1993), which strong compositional shifts in other taxa can violate. V9 tests this directly.
- Covariate-adjusted calibration (Freedman–Lane permutation, as reviewed by Winkler et al. 2014, or PIMA-style sign-flip score tests) is deferred to v3.1.

### 27.4 Scope and compute

- **Calibrated on the web:** Quick mode (four elementary tests) plus ALDEx2, whose Monte-Carlo CLR instances are label-independent so its tests vectorise too.
- **Offline only:** ANCOM-BC and PyDESeq2 re-estimate with labels, so they are excluded from web calibration. Offline they run with B ≤ 500 plus the tail fit.
- **Budget target:** calibrated Quick run on the IBD demo (120 × 380, 1,560 specifications, B = 2,000) in ≤ 60 s on 2 vCPU, plus the v2 run time. Measure and record in `docs/benchmark.json`.
- **Optional:** sequential Monte-Carlo early stopping for clearly null taxa (Besag & Clifford 1991).

### 27.5 Interface

- The results table gets a "Calibrated" column group (q_j, CR_j, CERTIFIED ROBUST).
- The specification curve marks certified-rejected specifications.
- The methods paragraph states the error-control guarantee and its assumptions in two plain sentences.

### 27.6 Acceptance criteria

- Vectorised statistics equal the existing scalar implementations to 1e-10 for every test on random data.
- Under simulated nulls, family p-values are uniform (KS p > 0.01 across 200 replicates).
- V9 (§33) meets its pre-registered thresholds, or the failure mode is documented and claims are restricted accordingly.
- With calibration off, v2 outputs are unchanged.

## §28 Phase 2: Practice-weighted multiverse and enumerated m-value (N2)

### 28.1 Why

Equal weighting of defensible pipelines is itself a choice. Miao, Pritchard & Zou (2026) define the m-value: the probability that an analysis path drawn from a declared distribution Π produces a claim at least as extreme as the reported one. They estimate Π by letting AI agents sample analysis paths.

For microbiome DA, Π can be measured instead of simulated, because pipelines are written in methods sections. Kleine Bardenhorst et al. (2021) screened 1,444 human microbiome publications, included 419, and found that heterogeneity in methods was largest at the differential abundance step. That is the data source for an empirical, reproducible, non-LLM Π.

### 28.2 Systematic practice review (human-led; the code supports it)

- **Protocol.** Register on OSF before extraction; report with a PRISMA 2020 flow diagram.
- **Corpus A.** Kleine Bardenhorst et al. (2021) extraction covering 2018–2019, if their supplementary data are usable. Otherwise re-extract a random sample of their included studies.
- **Corpus B.** A new random sample of 250 human two-group case-control DA papers, 2022–2026 (PubMed / PMC open access; 16S and shotgun).
- **Codebook** (`docs/practice/codebook.md`). Fields:
  - rarefied (Y/N, depth);
  - prevalence and abundance filters (thresholds);
  - normalisation or transformation;
  - DA method(s) and software;
  - multiple-testing correction (method, threshold, or none);
  - taxonomic rank analysed;
  - covariates;
  - reported significant taxa with direction;
  - data availability (accession).
- **Reliability.** A second coder double-codes 20% of papers. Report Cohen's κ per field.
- **LLM assistance.** LLM-assisted pre-extraction is allowed only with human verification of every field. Log which fields were machine-suggested.
- **Outputs.** `data/practice/extractions.csv`, `docs/practice/practice_weights.json`, and a practice-frequency figure for `/about`.

### 28.3 Two reference classes

| Class | Grid | Weights | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Defensible Π (default) | v3 grid after pruning | `decision_tree` (§26) | Tiers, certification, m-values |
| Practised Π | v3 grid plus **practice-only levels**: unadjusted p < 0.05; a LEfSe-style Kruskal–Wallis on TSS plus LDA filter (Segata et al. 2011) | `practice` tree (§26.2) | m-values and the coverage audit only; **never** tiers or certification |

### 28.4 Coverage audit

- Map each extracted pipeline to grid levels.
- Report three shares: fully inside the defensible grid, inside the practised grid, and outside both.
- List the ten most common out-of-grid choices.
- Target: ≥ 80% inside the practised grid. If coverage is below target, add the missing levels (with a defensibility tag) before running V11.

This answers the standard critique of any multiverse, "who decided the grid?", with data.

### 28.5 Enumerated m-value (`app/core/practice.py`)

- For a declared pipeline s0 and signed claim statistic T (signed z), the weighted two-sided m-value over the chosen Π is:
  `m = 2 · min( Σ_s w_s·1[T_s ≤ t0], Σ_s w_s·1[T_s ≥ t0] )`
- For a significance claim (T = −log10 q, where larger is more extreme), use the upper tail: `m = Σ_s w_s·1[T_s ≥ t0]`.
- Report m under both the defensible and practised Π.
- v2's "Locate my result" becomes this m-value; keep the percentile as a secondary display.
- Wording is neutral: "your pipeline sits in the most extreme X% of defensible analyses for this taxon". Never accusatory, and no "best" pipeline is suggested.

### 28.6 Acceptance criteria

- κ ≥ 0.7 on rarefied, method and correction fields.
- Coverage ≥ 80% (or the grid is extended and the audit re-run).
- Calibration: when s0 is drawn from Π, m is uniform in simulation (KS p > 0.01).
- The practised Π never feeds tiers or certification (unit test).

## §29 Phase 3: Cross-cohort meta-multiverse and transportability (N3)

### 29.1 Why

Across 19 independent cohorts of five diseases, v2 tiers did not predict replication (13% vs 14%). Differences in population, protocol and sequencing outweigh the analytical choices MicroVerse varies. The open gap is a cross-cohort robustness measure that predicts replication in a new cohort.

Two strands of prior work point the way, and neither covers it:
- Voracek, Kossmeier & Tran (2019) adapted specification curves and multiverse analysis to the meta-analytic level (1,592 meta-analytic specifications in their worked example).
- MMUPHin (Ma et al. 2022) provides covariate-controlled batch correction and meta-analytic DA testing for microbial communities, but applies a single analytical pipeline per analysis.

### 29.2 Input

- Multiple cohorts of the same two-group contrast: several uploads with a cohort column, or Atlas cohorts (§32).
- Minimum 3 cohorts, each passing v2 input rules.

### 29.3 Integration forks (new)

| Fork | Levels | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| I1 integration | REML random-effects meta-analysis of per-cohort coefficients; DerSimonian–Laird random-effects (sensitivity); fixed-effect inverse-variance; weighted Stouffer of per-cohort signed z (√n weights); pooled "mega-analysis" with cohort as covariate; pooled after batch correction (MMUPHin `adjust_batch`, ConQuR) | Batch-correction levels are offline/Atlas only because they need R |
| I2 cohort inclusion | All subsets with ≥ k cohorts (k = 3) when there are ≤ 8 cohorts; otherwise leave-one-out and leave-two-out | Combinatorial meta-analysis in the sense of Voracek et al. 2019 |
| Per-cohort forks | The v3 grid (Quick by default) | |

- Coefficient-producing specifications (linear, logistic, LinDA, MaAsLin 3-style) feed the meta-analytic levels.
- Rank tests feed Stouffer only. This is declared in the defensibility register.
- Meta-specification weights extend the §26 decision tree with an integration node and a cohort-subset node, each uniform over valid children.

### 29.4 Metrics and label

Per taxon:
- **Meta-robustness:** weighted share of meta-specifications significant at q ≤ α with consistent sign.
- **Heterogeneity:** median I².
- **Prediction-interval exclusion:** share of random-effects meta-specifications whose 95% prediction interval excludes 0. Prediction intervals describe where the effect in a new population is expected to lie, which is exactly the transportability question (Riley, Higgins & Deeks 2011).

**TRANSPORTABLE** requires all three:
- meta-robustness ≥ 0.8;
- sign agreement ≥ 0.95;
- prediction-interval exclusion ≥ 0.5.

Thresholds are pre-registered in V10 and not tuned afterwards.

### 29.5 Implementation notes

- REML random-effects in Python (scipy optimisation). Validate against R `metafor` (Viechtbauer 2010) on 100 random meta-analyses: τ² and pooled estimates within 1e-6 relative.
- Runs as a `meta` job; the Atlas precomputes diseases.
- Web path: no R. MMUPHin and ConQuR levels appear only in Atlas outputs.

### 29.6 Acceptance criteria

- `metafor` agreement as above.
- The LOCO pipeline is reproducible from one command.
- V10 is run and reported whichever way it falls.

## §30 Phase 4: Method coverage and modelling upgrades

Every method follows the v2 pattern:
- a Python implementation in the web path;
- a reference-validation script in `tests/reference/` that compares it with the R package offline;
- the script is registered in `run_all.py` and reports SKIPPED with an install hint when R is absent.

| ID | Addition | Detail | Reference target |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30.1 | LinDA (Zhou et al. 2022) | Linear model on CLR with mode-based bias correction of coefficients; two-group case | Effect r ≥ 0.99 vs R; significant-set Jaccard ≥ 0.9, or documented deviation |
| 30.2 | MaAsLin 3-style dual model (Nickols et al. 2026) | Per taxon: logistic prevalence model with log depth, plus linear model on log relative abundance of non-zeros; separate prevalence and abundance results | Agreement with R `maaslin3` on the demo and 3 real cohorts |
| 30.3 | ANCOM-BC2 (Lin & Peddada 2024), two-group | Bias correction, variance regularisation, pseudo-count sensitivity filter; Full mode | Agreement with R `ANCOMBC::ancombc2` |
| 30.4 | Differential prevalence track | Separate weighted tiers for prevalence and abundance, e.g. "ROBUST in prevalence, FRAGILE in abundance". Motivated by evidence that presence/absence carries much of the signal (Pelto et al. 2025) and by MaAsLin 3's separation of the two | Unit tests; demo with planted prevalence-only effects |
| 30.5 | Pooled rarefaction level | R = 50 draws at min depth; statistic = mean of per-draw statistics; p-value from the §27 permutation null (never by averaging p-values). Repeated rarefying with pooled statistics is described as the correct implementation of rarefaction in a recent methods protocol (Springer Protocols, "Statistical Analysis of Microbiome Data"), in line with Schloss 2024 | Available only in calibrated runs |

### 31.1 Robustness-power planner (N4; `app/core/sim.py`, `app/core/planner.py`)

**Why it is new.** Existing microbiome power tools compute power for a single test, e.g. PERMANOVA-based power for distance-based analyses (Kelly et al. 2015) or a case-control web calculator (Mattiello et al. 2016). Recent design work makes sample sizes robust to data-generating assumptions (Hagar & Martin, arXiv:2512.16231), not to analytical choices. No tool estimates the probability that an effect is found robust across the analytical multiverse. v2 already showed that cohorts under about 40 per group produce no ROBUST or CONDITIONAL calls; the planner turns that into prospective guidance.

**Template.** Control-group samples from a pilot upload, or an Atlas cohort.

**Simulator.** A Python port of MIDASim's nonparametric mode (He, Zhao & Satten 2024), which works in two steps:
1. correlated presence–absence indicators via a Gaussian copula;
2. relative abundances for present taxa with empirical marginals, again coupled by a Gaussian copula.

Effect injection:
- a multiplicative fold change on chosen taxa, followed by renormalisation, so compositional side-effects are realistic;
- an optional prevalence shift;
- library sizes resampled from the template.

**Grid.** n per group ∈ {20, 30, 40, 60, 80, 100, 150, 200}; R = 50 simulated datasets per n on the web, 200 offline. Effects are stratified by fold change (1.5, 2, 4) and prevalence (low, mid, high).

**Outputs.**
- Robustness-power curves: P(ROBUST), P(CERTIFIED ROBUST) and P(at least CONDITIONAL) against n.
- The false-ROBUST rate among null taxa.
- The smallest n giving 80% robustness-power per stratum.
- Plain-language summary. Format example with illustrative numbers only: "with 60 per group, a 2-fold change in a genus present in 30% of samples is found ROBUST in about 7 of 10 studies".

**Validation.**
- The simulator is compared with R MIDASim on summary statistics (taxon means, prevalence, taxon–taxon correlation, library sizes).
- Planner predictions are compared with subsampled real cohorts (V12).

### 31.2 Sealed multiverse pre-registration (N5; `app/core/seal.py`)

1. **Build.** Before analysis, the user builds a plan: contrast, forks and levels, weighting scheme, tier thresholds, calibration settings (B, q), optional primary taxa hypotheses, and seed.
2. **Seal.** The plan is serialised as canonical JSON (sorted keys, UTF-8, no insignificant whitespace) and hashed with SHA-256. The user downloads the JSON plus a human-readable summary and deposits them on OSF (URL field in the plan).
3. **Check at run time.** The user uploads the sealed plan. The engine flags any deviation and requires explicit confirmation. The results page shows either "matches sealed plan <hash>" or a line-by-line diff of deviations.
4. **Stated limitation.** The tool cannot prove the plan predates data access; its value is transparency, as with preregistration generally (Nosek et al. 2018).

### 31.3 Claim audit / reviewer mode (N6; `app/core/audit.py`)

**Inputs.** A dataset plus one or more claims (taxon, direction, declared pipeline).

**Outputs.**
- v2 tier under every weighting scheme;
- calibrated q_j and CR_j;
- m-value under the defensible and practised Π;
- the forks that flip the claim, from the measured one-at-a-time view;
- Atlas context.

The output is a one-page, neutrally worded report for authors, reviewers and editors. Like the rest of MicroVerse, it never recommends a pipeline.

### 31.4 Reporting pack (`app/core/report.py`)

- Methods and results paragraphs with citations, describing the multiverse, the weighting scheme, calibration settings and guarantees.
- A checklist of which STORMS items the run satisfies (Mirzayi et al. 2021).
- A figure panel: specification curve, tier and calibration table, weighting sensitivity.
- A machine-readable JSON of every output.
- An RO-Crate bundle (Soiland-Reyes et al. 2022) with input hashes, plan hash, software versions and seeds.
- A robustness statement template. Format example with illustrative numbers only: "Of 312 genera tested, 4 were ROBUST (3 CERTIFIED ROBUST at q = 0.05; decision-tree weights); the declared pipeline's m-value for Faecalibacterium was 0.04."

### 31.5 Atlas lookup inside results (after Phase 6)

Each taxon row links to its Atlas label for the same disease and to its taxon page.

## §32 Phase 6: MicroVerse Atlas (N7; `atlas/`)

### 32.1 Why

GMrepo v2 curates 353 projects and 71,642 samples and lists 592 marker taxa for 47 phenotype pairs, identified per project from 83 selected projects and then compared across datasets. Those markers are not assessed for sensitivity to analytical choices. Tierney et al. (2022) measured robustness to covariate choices across 15 cohorts and 6 phenotypes, and found substantial sign inconsistency for 1 in 3 of the 581 literature-reported associations they checked.

No resource reports, for each microbiome–disease association, how robust it is to the full space of analytical choices, with error control, and whether it transports across cohorts. The Atlas is that resource.

### 32.2 Data sources (`atlas/sources.yaml`)

Record licence, version, citation and download checksum for each source:

| Source | Content | Licence note |
|---|---|---|
| curatedMetagenomicData (Pasolli et al. 2017) | Shotgun | Check each study |
| MicrobiomeHD (Duvallet et al. 2017) | 16S | CC-BY-NC-4.0: Atlas-derived data must stay non-commercial |
| Nearing et al. 2022 datasets | 16S | figshare, CC BY 4.0 |
| Pelto et al. 2025 curated cohorts | 16S and shotgun | Zenodo; check licence |

### 32.3 Taxonomy harmonisation

- A curated genus-level synonym table (GTDB vs NCBI naming, reclassified genera), versioned.
- Source-native ranks are kept too.
- Never merge profilers' outputs into one table without a cohort label.

### 32.4 Pipeline

- `atlas/run.py` is deterministic, seeded and resumable, with `make atlas` targets.
- Per cohort: Full grid plus calibrated Quick.
- Per disease with ≥ 3 cohorts: meta-multiverse (§29), including MMUPHin and ConQuR levels, run offline with R.
- Outputs are Parquet (full) and static JSON (web). There is no heavy compute at request time.

### 32.5 Views

- **Disease page:** taxa × labels × cohorts.
- **Taxon page:** labels across diseases.
- **Cohort page.**
- **Claim check:** "Is <taxon> <up/down> in <disease> robust and transportable?"
- **Downloads and releases:** versioned, each with a Zenodo DOI.

### 32.6 Literature cross-reference

- Map GMrepo v2 markers and Disbiome entries (Janssens et al. 2018) to Atlas labels.
- Headline table: which literature markers are TRANSPORTABLE, ROBUST-but-not-transportable, or FRAGILE.
- For taxon-set interpretation of Atlas results, link out to TaxSEA (taxon set enrichment against public databases) rather than rebuilding it.

### 32.7 Acceptance criteria

- Atlas v1.0 covers ≥ 60 case-control cohorts across ≥ 8 diseases.
- Every number is reproducible via `make atlas`.
- For overlapping cohorts and phenotypes, a comparison with Tierney et al. (2022) robust/non-robust calls is reported.

## §40 Draft related-work paragraph (for P1; update after a fresh literature sweep)

Analytical flexibility in microbiome differential abundance (DA) analysis is well documented. Fourteen DA methods applied to 38 datasets identified markedly different sets of taxa (Nearing et al., 2022), and elementary methods gave the most replicable results across 53 studies (Pelto et al., 2025). A systematic review of 419 human microbiome studies found that methodological heterogeneity was greatest at the DA step (Kleine Bardenhorst et al., 2021). Vibration-of-effects analyses fitting millions of models showed that covariate choices alone can reverse the direction of microbiome–disease associations (Tierney et al., 2021, 2022).

Tools that respond to this problem either help analysts choose a single method (DAtest; benchdamic) or merge several methods into one consensus call (dar; nf-core/daamicrobiome). Outside microbiome research, multiverse and specification-curve analysis report the full distribution of defensible results (Steegen et al., 2016; Simonsohn et al., 2020). Recent work adds inference with family-wise error control (PIMA; Girardi et al., 2024), false discovery rate control via e-values (Rognon-Vael & Rossell, 2026), meta-analytic multiverses (Voracek et al., 2019), and the m-value, which locates a reported claim within the distribution of analyses that could have been reported (Miao et al., 2026).

To our knowledge, no tool combines the following for microbiome DA:
- an enumerated multiverse over preprocessing, method, correction and covariate choices;
- calibrated error control across both taxa and pipelines;
- an empirically derived, literature-based weighting of pipelines;
- a test of whether findings transport across cohorts.

MicroVerse v3 is designed to fill that gap.
