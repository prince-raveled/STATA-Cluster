4

Inside one specification

Each specification applies its choices in this fixed order. The order is enforced in one place in the engine: filtering before rarefying, for example, gives a different answer.

  1. RarefySubsample each sample to one depth, or not at all.
  2. CollapseKeep the input rank, or sum features to genus.
  3. FilterDrop taxa seen in too few samples ({{ dfn('prevalence_filter', 'prevalence') }}).
  4. TransformTSS, CLR {{ help('clr') }}, raw counts or TMM {{ help('tmm') }}.
  5. TestOne statistical test compares the groups: a {{ dfn('p_value', 'p-value') }} per taxon.
  6. CorrectBH or BY across the taxa tested: an {{ dfn('q_value', 'adjusted p-value') }}.
  7. CallSignificant when the adjusted p-value is at or below the threshold.

Effect size, computed alongside: the log2 fold change of mean relative abundance ({{ summary.group_b }} relative to {{ summary.group_a }}), taken from the matrix after step 4 with one pseudocount for the whole run. It is the same whichever test ran and whichever covariates were adjusted for, so every specification can share one axis.

5

Across the specification space

After the run, each taxon has one result from every specification that tested it. Those results are summarised, not averaged into a single p-value.

{{ dfn('significance_rate', 'How often significant') }}

The share of the specifications that tested the taxon and called it significant. Taxa removed by a filter are not counted against it.

{{ dfn('direction_agreement') }}

The share of those specifications that agree on which group the taxon is higher in.

{{ dfn('tier', 'Robustness label') }}

Those two numbers decide the label, from ROBUST to UNSTABLE. A second analysis then attributes the variation to the choices that caused it.
