01

What MicroVerse does

It takes one abundance table and two groups, enumerates every defensible way of testing which taxa differ, removes the incoherent combinations, runs the rest, and reports for each taxon how often it was significant, whether the analyses agree on its direction, and which choice moved the answer. It does not pick a winner.

02

The problem

A differential abundance analysis involves at least six choices with no field consensus. Make one set and report one p-value, and you have reported a sample of size one from a distribution you never looked at.

6,035,110models fitted across 15 cohorts
1 in 3taxa flipped the sign of their association
84%of published associations recoverable as significant by picking the model

Tierney BT et al., PLOS Biology 2022;20(3):e3001556 — the study MicroVerse generalises.

03

A specification is one complete analysis

One value for every contested choice, applied in a fixed order, producing one p-value and one effect size per taxon. The {{ dfn('multiverse') }} is the set of all of them. Nothing is averaged across specifications: each one stays a separate answer, and it is the distribution of answers that is reported.

04

The seven choices, each genuinely contested

Every level below is defended in current literature. MicroVerse does not pick between them; it runs them all and reports what changes.

ChoiceLevelsWhy it is contested
1 · Rarefaction depth none · min · 1000 · 5000 · 10000, each × 3 seeds A twelve-year unresolved argument. McMurdie & Holmes 2014 called rarefying “statistically inadmissible”; Cameron et al. 2022 showed it guarantees Type I error control in permutation tests; Schloss 2024 argued it is currently the best approach. Each random draw is its own specification, never averaged: averaging p-values across draws is statistically wrong, and keeping them separate is what makes the draw's own contribution measurable.
2 · Prevalence filter 0% · 5% · 10% · 20% No standard exists for how rare is too rare. It changes both the taxa tested and the multiple-testing burden.
3 · Transformation TSS · CLR · raw · TMM The compositional school holds that CLR {{ help('clr') }} is not optional; count-model advocates hold that raw counts with a proper model are required. CLR uses multiplicative zero replacement rather than a fixed pseudocount, which would destroy the ratios CLR exists to preserve.
4 · Taxonomic rank genus · input rank Wang 2023 showed results depend heavily on the analysis unit, with a real resolution-versus-power trade-off. If your table is already at genus, this choice has one level and the grid halves, which MicroVerse reports rather than hides.
5 · Statistical test Wilcoxon · Welch's t · logistic · linear · ANCOM-BC · ALDEx2 · PyDESeq2 Nearing et al. 2022 compared 14 methods over 38 datasets and found materially different results. Pelto et al. 2025 found the elementary methods the most replicable, which is why Quick mode uses exactly those four, and says so.
6 · Multiple-testing correction BH at 0.05 · BH at 0.10 · BY at 0.05 Published studies use 0.05, 0.07 and 0.15, and BH versus BY, inconsistently. Applied afterwards to stored p-values, so all three settings cost essentially nothing.
7 · Covariates every subset (Covariate mode) Tierney et al. 2022 found BMI, age, sequencing depth and gender all changed both the size and the direction of associations, disease-specifically.

05

Pruning: what is ruled out, and why

Some combinations contradict themselves. They are enumerated, counted and removed with a stated reason, so every run reports both how many specifications exist and how many are coherent. The data can also rule things out: a table of relative abundances cannot be rarefied, and without a taxonomy nothing can be collapsed to genus.

07

Inside one specification

The order is fixed and enforced in one place. Rarefying after collapsing, or filtering before rarefying, gives different answers.

  1. RarefySubsample to one depth, or not at all.
  2. CollapseInput rank, or sum to genus.
  3. FilterDrop taxa below the prevalence threshold.
  4. TransformTSS, CLR, raw counts or TMM.
  5. TestA {{ dfn('p_value', 'p-value') }} per taxon.
  6. CorrectBH or BY: an {{ dfn('q_value', 'adjusted p-value') }}.
  7. CallSignificant at or below the threshold.

08

One effect size for every test

Seven methods return seven incompatible statistics: a Wilcoxon rank statistic, a log odds ratio and a DESeq2 log2 fold change cannot share an axis, so there would be no specification curve. Significance therefore comes from each method's own p-value, and the effect from a single estimator, the log2 fold change of mean relative abundance, computed identically for every specification with one pseudocount fixed for the whole run. Each method's native statistic is kept in the exports.

{% call callout('blue', 'A consequence worth knowing') %}

Because it comes from the table rather than a model, this effect does not change when an analysis adjusts for a covariate. Covariate effects show up in whether a taxon is called significant, not on the effect axis.

{% endcall %}

09

Two numbers decide the label

How often a taxon is significant after correction, and how consistently its effect points the same way. Both are computed over the specifications in which the taxon actually survived the prevalence filter, never over the run total. The rules are checked in this order, and the first match wins.

LabelMeaningRule

Why the denominator matters

A taxon removed by a 20% prevalence filter does not appear in that specification at all. Counting it as “not significant” there would silently punish every rare taxon. So the share of the grid that tested a taxon is reported as its own finding: a taxon testable in only 30% of the grid is filter-dependent by construction.

Are the labels meaningful?

They were tested against held-out halves of published cohorts: the label assigned on one half predicts replication on the other (AUC {{ TIER_VALIDATION.auc }}). The full evidence.

10

Which choice moved the answer

Two views. Per taxon, MicroVerse changes one choice at a time with everything else held fixed and measures how far the significance rate moves; that is measured, not modelled. Across the run, it apportions the variation in results among the choices (Young & Holsteen 2017) with a mixed-effects model and an independent fallback estimator beside it. That second view is graded exploratory, and the two estimates are always shown together so a disagreement is visible.

11

A multiverse tool can be used to p-hack. This one is built not to be.

  • There is no “export best specification” button: not on the page, not in the API, not in the download bundle.
  • Every export contains the full distribution. Even the thinning on the specification curve takes an even stride, so the shape survives.
  • The generated methods paragraph describes the multiverse, not a point in it.
  • If you declare your own pipeline and it sits above the 75th percentile of significance, the interface says so, neutrally.

12

Scope and limitations

A tool that does one thing completely is more useful than one that does six things partially. These are refusals, not gaps.

Out of scope

  • Two-group comparisons only. No multi-group, continuous or ordinal outcomes; no longitudinal or paired designs.
  • Input starts at the abundance table. No read processing, denoising or taxonomic classification.
  • No diversity metrics and no contamination screening.
  • No accounts. Each run gets an unguessable link; uploads and results are deleted after {{ retention_days }} days.
{% call callout('red', 'What MicroVerse is not') %}
  • Not a new statistical method. It orchestrates published methods inside published frameworks: multiverse analysis (Steegen 2016), specification curves (Simonsohn 2020), vibration of effects (Patel 2015).
  • Not truth. A taxon robust to analytical choice is not thereby biologically real.
  • Not exhaustive. The space is bounded by what is defensible and computable, and every run reports what it enumerated, pruned and why.
  • Not a replacement for judgement. The scientific decision stays with you, and so does the obligation to report it honestly.
{% endcall %}
