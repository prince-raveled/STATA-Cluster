Method

Report the distribution, not your favourite point in it.

A differential abundance analysis involves at least six choices with no field consensus. If you make one set and report one p-value, you have reported a sample of size one from a distribution you never looked at.

01

Tierney et al. showed that 84% of published microbiome associations could be recovered as significant by picking the right model, and that one taxon in three flipped the sign of its association across defensible choices. MicroVerse exists so that looking at the distribution is cheap enough that there is no excuse not to.

The grid of analyses

Seven forks, each genuinely contested.

Every level below is defended in current literature. MicroVerse does not pick between them; it runs them all and reports what changes.

Fork Levels Why it is contested
1 · Rarefaction depth none · min · 1000 · 5000 · 10000, each × 3 seeds A twelve-year unresolved argument. McMurdie & Holmes 2014 called rarefying “statistically inadmissible”; Cameron et al. 2022 showed it guarantees Type I error control in permutation tests; Schloss 2024 argued it is currently the best approach. Each random draw is its own specification, never averaged — averaging p-values across draws is statistically wrong, and keeping them separate is what makes the draw's own contribution measurable.
2 · Prevalence filter 0% · 5% · 10% · 20% No standard exists for how rare is too rare. It changes both the taxa tested and the multiple-testing burden.
3 · Transformation TSS · CLR · raw · TMM The compositional school holds that CLR is not optional; count-model advocates hold that raw counts with a proper model are required. Both publish continuously. CLR uses multiplicative zero replacement rather than a fixed pseudocount, which would destroy the ratios CLR exists to preserve.
4 · Taxonomic rank genus · input rank Wang 2023 showed results depend heavily on the analysis unit, with a real resolution-versus-power trade-off. If your table is already at genus, this fork has one level and the grid halves — which MicroVerse reports rather than hides.
5 · DA method Wilcoxon · Welch's t · logistic · linear · ANCOM-BC · ALDEx2 · PyDESeq2 Nearing et al. 2022 compared 14 methods over 38 datasets and found materially different results. Pelto et al. 2025 found the elementary methods the most replicable — which is why Quick mode uses exactly those four, and says so.
6 · FDR correction BH@0.05 · BH@0.10 · BY@0.05 Published studies use 0.05, 0.07 and 0.15, and BH versus BY, inconsistently. Applied post-hoc to stored p-values, so all three settings cost essentially nothing.
7 · Covariates every subset (covariate mode) Tierney et al. 2022 found BMI, age, sequencing depth and gender all changed both the size and the direction of associations, disease-specifically.

The pipeline order is fixed and enforced in one place: rarefy → collapse → prevalence filter → transform → test → FDR. Rarefying after collapsing, or filtering before rarefying, gives different answers.

How a taxon gets its verdict

Two numbers decide the tier.

How often a taxon is FDR-significant, and how consistently its effect points the same way. Both are computed over the specifications in which it actually survived the prevalence filter — never over the run total.

Why the denominator matters

A taxon removed by a 20% prevalence filter does not appear in that specification at all. Counting it as “not significant” there would silently punish every rare taxon, and counting the specification in the denominator would understate how often the taxon was actually tested.

So MicroVerse reports frac_tested as its own finding: a taxon testable in only 30% of the grid is filter-dependent by construction, and you should know that before you read anything else about it.

Why effect size is computed separately

Seven methods return seven incompatible statistics. A Wilcoxon U, a log odds ratio and a DESeq2 log2 fold change cannot share an axis, so there would be no specification curve.

Significance therefore comes from each method's own p-value, and effect size from a single estimator — the log2 fold change of mean relative abundance — computed identically for every specification, with one pseudocount fixed for the whole run.

Designed against cherry-picking

A multiverse tool can be used to p-hack. This one is built not to be.

  • There is no “export best specification” button — not on the page, not in the API, not in the download bundle.
  • Every export contains the full distribution. Even the point-thinning on the specification curve takes an even stride, so the shape of the distribution survives.
  • The generated methods paragraph describes the multiverse, not a point in it.
  • If you declare your own pipeline and it sits above the 75th percentile of significance, the interface says so, neutrally.

What is in scope

What is deliberately out of scope.

A tool that does one thing completely is more useful than one that does six things partially. These are refusals, not gaps.

  1. 01
    Two-group comparisons only No multi-group, continuous or ordinal outcomes. No longitudinal or paired designs, no repeated measures.
  2. 02
    Input starts at the abundance table No read processing, no denoising, no taxonomic classification.
  3. 03
    No diversity metrics, no contamination screening Alpha and beta diversity are not standalone features here, and contamination assessment is a separate problem with a separate tool.
  4. 04
    No accounts, no login, no saved projects Each run gets an unguessable token URL. Anyone with the link sees the results; nobody without it does. Uploads and results are deleted after {{ retention_days }} days.

Stated plainly

What MicroVerse is not.

Not a new statistical method. It orchestrates published methods inside a published framework — multiverse analysis (Steegen 2016), specification curve analysis (Simonsohn 2020) and vibration of effects (Patel 2015).

Not truth. A taxon robust to analytical choice is not thereby biologically real. Confounding, contamination and batch effects survive a multiverse intact.

Not exhaustive. The grid is bounded by what is defensible and computable. Every run reports how many specifications it enumerated, how many it pruned, and why.

Not a replacement for judgement. MicroVerse ranks and describes; the scientific decision stays with you, and so does the obligation to report it honestly.
